# Orbitkeep v0.5 Flight Director orchestration specification

Status: required, not yet implemented

Target release: Orbitkeep v0.5.0

Release disposition: blocking

## Product boundary

Claude and Codex provide the intelligence used to reason about and perform a
Mission. Orbitkeep is the provider-neutral control plane around that
intelligence. It supplies identity, policy, permissions, Executive Clearance,
workflow state, action mediation, lifecycle enforcement, evidence, recovery,
and operator controls.

Orbitkeep must preserve useful model autonomy. It does not pre-script every
implementation decision or preassign every Mission Specialist in the Flight
Plan. It confines autonomous action to the approved Mission, effective Charter,
declared capabilities, filesystem and network boundaries, resource limits,
and observable lifecycle transitions.

The v0.5 boundary is a local policy and process-control envelope. It is not an
OS security sandbox. Docker-backed process, filesystem, network, resource, and
secret isolation remains v0.7 Mission Module scope and must not be implied by
v0.5 documentation or diagnostics.

## Roles and authority

### Executive

The Executive supplies the objective, approves or steers the Flight Plan,
answers escalations, controls material Course Corrections, and accepts or
rejects the final Mission Report.

### Flight Director

The provider manager is the Flight Director. After approval it remains the
reasoning, staffing, supervision, integration, and reporting authority for the
Mission. It:

- interprets the approved Flight Plan as an outcome and boundary, not a fixed
  Crew schedule;
- decides when a task is economical to perform directly and when specialist
  expertise is warranted;
- dynamically selects enabled Mission Specialist roles and permitted models;
- prepares the semantic content of each Mission Brief;
- supervises active Crew and evaluates their Mission Reports;
- accepts work for integration, requests rework, or dispatches independent
  review and validation;
- owns the integration and merge lane;
- reports status and raises side questions or material escalations to the
  Executive; and
- declares when the Mission is ready for final Executive acceptance.

The Flight Director does not grant Executive Clearance, expand its own
authority, directly edit canonical state, receive ownership fencing tokens, or
represent provider-native activity as governed Crew without a validated
dispatch.

### Mission Specialist

A Mission Specialist receives one versioned canonical Mission Brief, performs
only that bounded work, and returns one structured Mission Report. A specialist
does not dispatch other specialists, mutate canonical workflow state, approve
its own work, merge, deploy, or expand its scope.

### Orbitkeep Core and supervisor

Orbitkeep does not make the manager's technical judgments. It authenticates,
validates, records, launches, observes, interrupts, and reconciles the actions
requested by the Flight Director. Canonical authority remains in Orbitkeep
Core; provider output is an untrusted request or observation until accepted by
the applicable transition.

## Flight Plan contract

The Flight Plan remains concise and human-readable. It defines the objective,
approach, acceptance criteria, material boundaries, and known constraints. It
may name a specialist or mandatory gate when that is materially important, but
it is not required to enumerate anticipated Crew assignments.

Normal role selection, sequencing, parallelization, review depth, and bounded
nonmaterial adjustments remain Flight Director decisions. A dispatch outside
the approved objective, material paths, permissions, risk posture, or
acceptance criteria requires a new Flight Plan revision and Executive
Clearance.

## Runtime model

After Clearance, Orbitkeep starts or resumes a durable Flight Director loop
bound to the Mission. The loop does not terminate merely because a specialist
was launched or returned. It continues until one of these states is reached:

- waiting for one or more Crew results;
- waiting for an Executive answer, Airlock decision, or Course Correction;
- paused, handing over, cancelled, or blocked;
- ready to submit the final Mission Report; or
- failed with a concrete recorded outcome.

The supervisor may reactivate the manager for a new turn when a Crew result,
Executive message, control request, or relevant failure arrives. Provider
session identifiers are resumability hints and provenance, not canonical
manager identity.

## Governed Crew dispatch

The Flight Director receives a provider-neutral dispatch capability rather
than raw workflow credentials. A request contains at least:

- requested canonical role and model selection or allowed selection policy;
- objective and expected outputs;
- affected scope and explicit exclusions;
- required read, write, network, secret, and command permissions;
- acceptance criteria and evidence requirements;
- known dependencies, gates, constraints, escalation conditions, and stopping
  conditions; and
- whether the work may safely run concurrently.

Orbitkeep authenticates the request to the active Flight Director session and
validates it against the active Flight Plan, Local and Central Charter,
configured role and model policy, Mission and Run budgets, dependencies,
quality gates, concurrency limits, and current Command Authority.

An accepted dispatch atomically creates or binds the canonical Operation, Run,
and versioned Mission Brief before launching the provider specialist. A denied
dispatch returns a stable reason to the manager and creates the required audit
evidence without granting partial authority. The model never receives the
Mission ownership fencing token.

## Specialist execution and result return

Provider-specific Docking Adapters translate the canonical role and Mission
Brief into a Claude or Codex specialist invocation without changing its
semantics. The selected role definition, requested model, granted permissions,
and canonical identifiers are fixed for that Run.

The specialist's completion produces a candidate Mission Report. Orbitkeep
validates and records delivery, then returns a bounded report and evidence
references to the Flight Director. Delivery does not imply acceptance. The
manager may:

- accept the report for integration;
- request rework with concrete feedback, producing a new Run;
- request independent review, security, QA, reliability, or other configured
  specialist work;
- escalate an ambiguity or material change; or
- mark the Operation blocked with a concrete cause.

The Flight Director's acceptance of specialist work is distinct from the
Executive's acceptance of the final Mission Report.

## Direct manager work and merge lane

The Flight Director may perform a small, low-risk one-off task when dispatch
overhead would be disproportionate. Direct work remains subject to the same
Flight Plan, Charter, Command Authority, tool authorization, evidence, and
materiality rules. The manager must not use this allowance to become the
default implementation worker or evade specialist controls.

The Flight Director alone owns integration and the merge lane. Until Docker
Mission Modules provide per-Run isolation, v0.5 must serialize overlapping
write scopes or use a verified local worktree/patch strategy. Parallel
read-only work is allowed; parallel writes require demonstrably disjoint scope
and post-Run workspace verification. A specialist never merges its own work.

## Executive interaction

The Flight Director provides concise status and exposes active Crew without
requiring the Executive to poll internal records. It may ask a side question
without pausing unrelated safe work. It must stop affected work and request a
new decision when a material Course Correction, new permission, deployment,
secret exposure, destructive action, or other configured Airlock is required.

Executive responses travel through the Orbitkeep parent control channel. A
managed provider process cannot impersonate the Executive or approve its own
request.

## Crew roster and observability

Every accepted dispatch appears in the provider-neutral active Crew roster
with canonical role, Operation, Run, selected model, lifecycle, start time,
last activity, and terminal outcome. Detach, reconnect, supervisor restart,
retry, and rework must reconcile without leaving false active Crew.

Provider-internal sub-agent activity without an accepted canonical dispatch is
reported only as observed provider activity. Orbitkeep must not invent a role,
Mission Brief, permissions, or canonical identity for it after the fact.

## Failure and recovery

- A failed dispatch creates no running Run and returns a concrete error.
- A launched specialist with an unknown outcome blocks dependent work until
  reconciliation produces `completed`, `failed`, `cancelled`, or `prevented`.
- A manager restart retains canonical Mission ownership rules and reconstructs
  its context from the active Flight Plan, Operations, Runs, reports,
  unresolved decisions, and recorded evidence.
- A provider session that cannot be resumed may be replaced by the same
  canonical Flight Director identity only through the brokered recovery path.
- Handover transfers durable Mission management after checkpointing; it does
  not claim to transfer live provider processes.
- No provider or model substitution occurs silently.

## v0.5 release qualification

Publication is blocked until both Claude and Codex pass live UAT demonstrating:

1. A human-readable Flight Plan that does not preassign all Crew.
2. Executive approval followed by a persistent Flight Director loop.
3. Manager-selected dispatch of at least two different configured specialist
   roles through the governed channel.
4. Canonical Mission Brief creation before each provider launch.
5. Concurrent read-only Crew and safely serialized or disjoint write work.
6. Specialist report return to the same manager context.
7. Manager acceptance, concrete rework, and a subsequent accepted Run.
8. Independent review or validation selected by the manager when warranted.
9. A material escalation that blocks only the affected work until the
   Executive responds.
10. Live Crew roster accuracy across detach and reconnect.
11. Manager-owned integration and final Mission Report submission.
12. Executive-only final acceptance and complete cleanup/reconciliation.
13. Rejection of a dispatch that exceeds scope, policy, model, permission,
    budget, or concurrency authority without partial canonical state.
14. Recovery from an interrupted specialist and an interrupted Flight Director
    without fabricating completion.

Automated unit and integration tests must cover the same state transitions,
authorization boundaries, malformed dispatches, duplicate delivery,
idempotency, failure, and recovery behavior. Provider-native sub-agent success
alone is not evidence of this contract.
